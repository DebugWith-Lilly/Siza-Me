My community. My Safety. My Voice.
SizaMe is a privacy-first community safety and gender-based violence
(GBV) support platform designed to help people document concerns, find
trusted support, and navigate the right response pathway from one place.

Developed by Team CtrlAltDefeat, SizaMe brings community incident
response and GBV support together in one connected concept, with safety,
privacy, and user choice at its core.
Project status: Prototype / concept in development. Features
described as planned are not necessarily implemented or validated.

The Problem
People experiencing community safety incidents or gender-based violence
may need to navigate different reporting channels, emergency services,
support organisations, and referral systems. This can make it difficult
to know where to turn, document incidents safely, or access appropriate
support---especially when someone is concerned about privacy,
retaliation, or being discovered.
Our Solution
SizaMe is designed around two connected pathways:
- Community Response --- for documenting and navigating community
  safety concerns.
- GBV Response --- for discreetly recording concerns and finding
  relevant support and referral options.
The aim is to make the journey from reporting to support easier to
understand, while avoiding unnecessary exposure of sensitive
information.
Key Features
The following features describe the intended product direction.
Availability depends on what is implemented in the current version of
the repository.
- Two response pathways: Community Response and GBV Response in
  one platform.
- Incident documentation: A private diary-style space to record
  reports and relevant details.
- Privacy-first reporting: Explore anonymous or discreet reporting
  options where technically and legally appropriate.
- Quick Exit: A discreet way to leave the app interface quickly.
  This should not be treated as a guarantee that device activity or
  browser history is hidden.
- Trusted services directory: Connect users with verified
  emergency, medical, legal, shelter, and support services.
- Emergency support: Clear pathways to appropriate emergency
  resources.
- Evidence organisation: A proposed secure space to organise
  incident-related notes or evidence, with careful access controls.
- Safety education and prevention: Information that helps users
  understand available support and safer next steps.
- Optional GPS tracker concept: The team has explored a reusable
  GPS/GNSS tracker with cellular connectivity and disposable
  skin-coloured adhesive patches. This is a proposed hardware concept,
  not a claim of an implemented or validated tracking product.
Who It Is For
- People seeking information or support after a community safety
  incident.
- People experiencing or concerned about gender-based violence.
- Community organisations and service providers that may offer
  verified support.
- Potential partners supporting prevention, referral, and community
  safety initiatives.
Design Principles
SizaMe is being shaped by these principles:
1. Privacy by design --- collect as little sensitive data as
   practical and protect information that users choose to provide.
2. Safety and discretion --- consider the risks that app
   visibility, notifications, location data, and stored records can
   create.
3. User choice --- make support pathways clear without pressuring
   users to disclose or report.
4. Accessibility --- aim for a clear, approachable interface that
   is easy to navigate.
5. Trust and verification --- provide service information that can
   be checked and kept up to date.
6. Responsible data handling --- design around informed consent,
   appropriate access controls, and applicable privacy laws, including
   South Africa's POPIA where relevant.

Safety, Privacy and Limitations
SizaMe is intended to support access to information and services; it is
not a substitute for emergency services, medical care, legal advice,
or specialist GBV support.
- Verify all helpline numbers, service listings, and referral details
  before launch.
- Do not use real survivors' personal information in development or
  testing.
- Test reporting, authentication, access control, data retention,
  deletion, and backup behaviour before handling sensitive
  information.
- Location sharing and any proposed tracker must be opt-in, clearly
  explained, securely designed, and assessed for misuse and
  personal-safety risks.
- A Quick Exit feature cannot guarantee that all traces of app use are
  removed from a device.
- Avoid claiming that reports are anonymous, encrypted, or securely
  stored unless those protections have been implemented and
  independently checked.
If someone is in immediate danger, they should contact local emergency
services or a trusted support organisation.
